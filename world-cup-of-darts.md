# World Cup of Darts: The PDC's Pairs Championship Explained

The PDC World Cup of Darts provides one of the most electric spectacles in world sport. As the only pairs tournament on the professional circuit, it strips away the solitary nature of championship darts and places national pride on the line in an intense team environment.

Here is the definitive guide to the tournament, featuring the modern 40-nation format, the complete £500,000 prize money schedule, official broadcast channels, and the full historical archive of past champions.

---

## What Is the World Cup of Darts?

The World Cup of Darts is an annual international tournament staged by the [Professional Darts Corporation](https://www.pdc.tv/). First introduced in 2010, the competition brings together two-player national teams representing nations across Europe, the Americas, Asia, Oceania, and Africa.

Unlike standard ranking tournaments where competitors play exclusively as solo competitors, the World Cup is built entirely around doubles play. Teammates share visits to the board, throwing alternate visits of three darts while coordinating setup shots, checkouts, and board management. 

This cooperative format generates unique psychological drama. Elite players accustomed to controlling their own rhythm must trust their partner's throwing cadence and adapt when an off-target dart disrupts a planned checkout sequence.

## 2026 Result in Brief

England captured their record-extending sixth World Cup of Darts crown on Sunday, 14 June 2026, defeating fierce rivals the Netherlands 10–5 in the final at Frankfurt's Eissporthalle.

The English pairing of world champion [Luke Littler](/players/luke-littler/) and world number one [Luke Humphries](/players/luke-humphries/) produced a dominant campaign throughout the weekend. After navigating tense knockout matches on Saturday and Sunday afternoon, the English duo fired a combined match average exceeding 102 to overpower the Dutch tandem of Michael van Gerwen and Gian van Veen in the decider.

Defending champions Northern Ireland, represented by Josh Rock and Daryl Gurney following their maiden triumph in 2025, bowed out at the semi-final stage after suffering a narrow 8–7 defeat to the Netherlands in a deciding leg.

The triumph cemented England's status as the most successful nation in World Cup history, breaking their tie of five titles with the Netherlands.

## Format Explained

The World Cup underwent an extensive format overhaul in 2023, expanding the field to 40 nations and transitioning the entire event into a pure doubles tournament with no individual singles matches.

### The Seedings and Group Phase

1. **Top Four Seeded Nations:** The four highest-ranked countries—determined by the combined world ranking of their two selected players on the two-year rolling PDC Order of Merit—receive automatic qualification directly into the Second Round (Last 16).
2. **The 36 Unseeded Nations:** The remaining 36 teams are drawn into 12 round-robin groups of three teams each (Groups A through L).
3. **Group Play:** Each nation plays the other two teams in their group across the opening two days. Matches are contested as best-of-seven legs.
4. **Advancement:** Only the team finishing top of each group qualifies for the knockout phase, joining the four seeded nations in the Last 16.

### The Knockout Phase

From the Last 16 onward, the tournament operates as a straight single-elimination bracket:

- **Second Round (Last 16):** Best of 15 legs (first to 8)
- **Quarter-Finals:** Best of 15 legs (first to 8)
- **Semi-Finals:** Best of 15 legs (first to 8)
- **Final:** Best of 19 legs (first to 10)

Because every single match is played in alternate-visit doubles, teammates cannot rely on individual singles rubbers to bail them out. Chemistry, tempo alignment, and mutual encouragement are vital attributes for any partnership aiming to lift the trophy.

## Venue: Eissporthalle, Frankfurt

Since 2022, the World Cup of Darts has established a permanent home at the **Eissporthalle in Frankfurt, Germany**. 

Known for its lively arena atmosphere and cavernous seating bowl, the Eissporthalle regularly welcomes thousands of passionate European darts supporters across four days of summer action. The 2026 championship took place from 11 to 14 June 2026, generating packed sessions and deafening support for home and visiting nations alike.

The German venue has proven an ideal European hub for international supporters, providing convenient rail and air connections for fans travelling from across the UK, Netherlands, Belgium, and Scandinavia.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the World Cup of Darts work?
The World Cup of Darts is a 40-nation pairs tournament where each country is represented by two players. The top four seeded nations receive a bye into the Last 16, while the remaining 36 teams compete across 12 round-robin groups of three. Group winners join the top four seeds in a straight knockout bracket, with all matches contested as doubles leg play.

### How many teams play in the World Cup of Darts?
Exactly 40 national teams compete in the modern PDC World Cup of Darts, featuring 80 players from across Europe, the Americas, Asia, Africa, and Oceania.

### Who won the 2026 World Cup of Darts?
England won the 2026 World Cup of Darts, represented by Luke Littler and Luke Humphries, defeating the Netherlands (Michael van Gerwen and Gian van Veen) 10–5 in the final on 14 June 2026.

### Where is the World Cup of Darts held?
The World Cup of Darts is staged at the Eissporthalle in Frankfurt, Germany, which has served as the tournament's permanent venue since 2022.

### Does World Cup of Darts prize money count towards the Order of Merit?
No. Because the World Cup of Darts is an invitational team event rather than an individual ranking major, prize money won does not count towards the two-year rolling PDC Order of Merit.

### Which country has won the most World Cup of Darts titles?
England holds the record for the most World Cup of Darts titles, having won the tournament six times (2012, 2013, 2015, 2016, 2024, and 2026).
