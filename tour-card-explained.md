# PDC Tour Card Explained: The 128-Card System Behind Professional Darts

Every season, darts fans hear commentators talk about players winning, defending, or losing their "Tour Card." Here is the definitive reference guide to the 128-card professional system: what the card is, how cards are allocated, and what holding one actually means.

---

## What Is a PDC Tour Card?

A **PDC Tour Card** is the official two-year playing license issued by the Professional Darts Corporation ([PDC](https://www.pdc.tv/)). It grants an individual direct playing rights on the professional circuit.

There are exactly **128 Tour Cards** in circulation at any given time. Holding one establishes a player as an official touring professional and a member of the Professional Darts Players Association ([PDPA](https://pdpa.co.uk/)).

The card guarantees free entry into all 30 non-televised Players Championship floor events each season, plus exclusive access to European Tour qualifiers.

For practical advice on entering tournaments and training routines, see our guide on [how to become a pro darts player](/players/how-to-become-pro/). This guide explains how the 128-card license system itself works.

---

## How Many Tour Cards Exist & Who Holds Them

The professional circuit operates with a fixed pool of **128 cards**. At the start of each season in January, cardholders fall into three groups:

| Category | Typical Count | Qualification Origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Top 64 Order of Merit** | 64 Players | Retained via two-year rolling prize-money rankings |
| **Year Two Cardholders** | ~30–35 Players | In second year of two-year card won at previous Q-School or feeder tours |
| **New Intake (Year One)** | ~30–35 Players | Fresh two-year cards won via current Q-School and secondary tour rankings |
| **Total Active Roster** | **128 Players** | **Fixed professional playing pool** |

Every new Tour Card is valid for two full calendar years. This gives debutants a protected 24-month window to climb into the world's top 64 without fear of immediate relegation after one season.

---

## How to Win a Tour Card

There are four sporting routes into the 128-player professional card pool:

### Top 64 of the PDC Order of Merit

The primary route to holding a card is performance-based retention on the [PDC Order of Merit](/tournament/order-of-merit/) — the official two-year rolling prize-money ranking.

Following the World Darts Championship each January, the Order of Merit updates. Every player ranked inside the top 64 automatically retains their card for the upcoming season. As long as a player stays in the top 64, they cannot lose their card.

### PDC Qualifying School (Q-School, held each January)

Qualifying School is the annual open event where all vacant Tour Cards (typically 30 to 35 each season) are contested. Q-School operates simultaneously across two regional zones:

- **UK Q-School:** For players from the UK, Republic of Ireland, and Gibraltar.
- **European Q-School:** For players from all other nations.

Each event features First Stage (open knockout rounds) and Final Stage (qualifiers plus exempt players). Cards go to daily outright winners, with remaining cards awarded via UK and European Q-School ranking tables.

### Challenge Tour / Development Tour (top finishers)

The PDC rewards season-long consistency across its sanctioned secondary circuits:

- **Winmau Challenge Tour:** For players who competed at Q-School but missed out on a card. The top two players on the final season Order of Merit earn two-year PDC Tour Cards.
- **Winmau Development Tour:** For emerging youth players aged 16 to 23. The top two players on the final season Order of Merit earn two-year PDC Tour Cards.

If a player in the top two of either tour has already secured a card elsewhere, the spot rolls down to the next eligible player on that tour's ranking list.

### Women's Series (top finishers)

The PDC Women's Series provides female players with a direct sporting pathway into professional darts. High-ranking players on the Women's Series Order of Merit earn qualifying places for televised majors and secondary circuit exemptions.

When Tour Card allocations or qualifying invitations are awarded through female circuit initiatives, places are distributed strictly on prize money won across the series, offering open merit-based progression.

---

## How Tour Cards Are Lost

A PDC Tour Card is never permanent. Because each card is valid for two years, players face a strict ranking cutoff at the end of their second season.

At the end of the World Darts Championship in January, the Order of Merit is finalized. Any cardholder who has completed Year Two of their card and ranks **outside the top 64** loses their Tour Card immediately.

When a player loses their card:
- They lose direct entry into Players Championship floor events and European Tour qualifiers.
- They lose full voting PDPA membership.
- They return to amateur status and must enter Q-School the following January to win a new card.

Former cardholders usually enter Q-School directly at the Final Stage, but they start with zero points. If they do not win a card, they can compete on the secondary Challenge Tour.

## What a Tour Card Gives You

A Tour Card provides valuable playing opportunities, but it also carries significant misconceptions:

| What a Tour Card Guarantees | What a Tour Card Does NOT Guarantee |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Direct Entry to 30 Players Championships:** Free entry into every ProTour floor event (£125,000 purse per event). | **No Guaranteed Salary:** Players receive £0 basic wage. A first-round defeat pays £0 prize money. |
| **European Tour Qualifiers:** Exclusive access to Tour Card Holder qualifiers for 14 European Tour events. | **No Expense Coverage:** Cardholders pay all their own travel, hotel, flight, and food expenses. |
| **PDPA Membership:** Player voting rights, welfare support, and representation. | **No World Championship Spot:** Cardholders must qualify independently via rankings or qualifiers. |
| **World Championship Qualifier Entry:** Access to the closed PDPA Tour Card Holder World Championship Qualifier. | **No Automatic Major Invitations:** Events like the Premier League require separate qualification. |

A Tour Card provides access to competitions, not guaranteed income. To see how players qualify for Alexandra Palace, read our complete guide to [World Darts Championship qualification](/tournament/qualification-explained/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many PDC Tour Cards exist in total?
There are exactly 128 PDC Tour Cards in circulation at any given time. These 128 cards represent the full roster of recognized touring professionals entitled to compete on the PDC ProTour.

### How long does a PDC Tour Card last?
A new PDC Tour Card is valid for two consecutive calendar seasons. Players must establish themselves in the world's top 64 by the end of their second season to retain their card automatically.

### Can a player lose their PDC Tour Card?
Yes. If a player completes the second year of their two-year Tour Card and is outside the top 64 of the official PDC Order of Merit, their card immediately expires. They must re-enter Q-School to regain professional status.

### Do PDC Tour Card holders get paid a salary?
No. The PDC operates on a pure prize-money meritocracy with no basic salary or guaranteed appearance fees. Cardholders earn money only by winning matches and claiming prize funds in tournaments, while paying their own travel and accommodation expenses.

### Does holding a Tour Card guarantee a spot at the World Darts Championship?
No. Holding a Tour Card grants entry into qualifying events, but does not guarantee a World Championship spot. Players must qualify via the main PDC Order of Merit, the ProTour Order of Merit, or the final PDPA Tour Card Holder Qualifier.

### What happens if a player loses their Tour Card?
When a player loses their Tour Card, they drop to amateur status. They can re-enter Q-School the following January. If they do not win a card, they are eligible to play on the secondary Winmau Challenge Tour and can receive reserve call-ups for ProTour events when full cardholders withdraw.
