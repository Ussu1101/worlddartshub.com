# PDC Major Tournaments Explained: The Complete Guide to Darts Majors

*Last updated: October 2026 | Verified against official PDC tournament schedules and Order of Merit regulations*

While the **PDC World Darts Championship** at London's Alexandra Palace is the undisputed pinnacle of the sport, professional darts operates an elite calendar of premier televised tournaments staged from January to November. 

Known collectively as **the Majors**, these high-stakes events showcase unique playing formats—from the brutal multi-stage endurance of the World Matchplay to the double-starting requirement of the World Grand Prix and the unseeded open draw of the UK Open.

Crucially, not every televised tournament carries the same weight. The darts calendar is strictly divided into **Premier Ranking Majors**, which count directly toward the official two-year [PDC Order of Merit](/tournament/order-of-merit/), and **Premier Non-Ranking Invitationals**, which offer massive prestige and financial payouts but zero world ranking points.

This guide provides a comprehensive breakdown of every major tournament on the PDC circuit, detailing their historical heritage, entry qualifications, playing formats, and the exclusive **Triple Crown of Darts**.

## Ranked Majors vs Non-Ranked Invitationals: Why It Matters

The distinction between ranking and non-ranking tournaments is fundamental to understanding professional darts:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
|                   PREMIER RANKING MAJORS                        |
|  • Prize money WON is added to the 2-Year PDC Order of Merit    |
|  • Determines Official World Seedings & Tour Card Survival      |
|  • Examples: World Championship, World Matchplay, Grand Prix    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
                                vs
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
|                 PREMIER INVITATIONAL EVENTS                     |
|  • Players earn lucrative prize money, but £0 is added to OoM   |
|  • Entry determined by invitation, selection, or specific criteria|
|  • Examples: Premier League Darts, The Masters, World Cup       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Because the [PDC Order of Merit](/tournament/order-of-merit/) is a rolling two-year ranking ledger, prize money won in a ranking major expires after 24 months. Winning a £100,000 title provides immediate protection in the world rankings, whereas winning the £275,000 Premier League title boosts a player's bank account without moving them up the official world ranking ladder.

## The "Big Four" Premier Ranking Majors

Outside of the festive World Championship at Alexandra Palace, four ranking majors represent the backbone of the PDC season:

### 1. The World Matchplay (Winter Gardens, Blackpool)
- **Heritage:** Staged since 1994 inside the opulent Empress Ballroom at Blackpool's Winter Gardens, the Matchplay is widely considered by players and purists as the finest darts tournament in the world.
- **The Phil Taylor Trophy:** Renamed in honor of the 16-time champion Phil Taylor, who dominated the Winter Gardens stage.
- **Unique Format:** Strictly **leg play** where matches cannot be won on a sudden-death leg. A player must win by **two clear legs**, producing epic overtime marathons in the mid-summer heat.
- **Qualification:** The world's top 16 from the main Order of Merit, joined by the top 16 non-qualified players from the single-season ProTour Order of Merit.

### 2. The World Grand Prix (Leicester / Dublin)
- **The Format Test:** The Grand Prix is the only televised tournament in professional darts played under [Double-In, Double-Out](/darts/double-in-double-out/) rules.
- **The Challenge:** Players cannot begin scoring until they hit a finishing double or the 50-point red Bullseye. A player who misses three darts at their starting double scores zero for that turn.
- **Match Structure:** Played in sets rather than legs, intensifying pressure and punishing slow starters.

### 3. The UK Open ("The FA Cup of Darts", Minehead)
- **No Seedings:** Unlike standard tournaments where top seeds are kept apart, the UK Open features a completely unseeded, multi-board draw after each round. World champions can—and often do—face each other in the early rounds.
- **Massive Field:** Features 160 competitors, combining PDC Tour Card holders, top Challenge and Development Tour amateurs, and regional pub qualifiers.
- **Staging:** Staged across eight simultaneous stages at Butlin's Minehead, offering a chaotic, festival atmosphere.

### 4. The Grand Slam of Darts (Wolverhampton)
- **The Format:** Combines a round-robin group stage (eight groups of four, best-of-9 legs) with a grueling knockout stage (best-of-19 and best-of-31 legs).
- **Heritage:** Awarded the **Eric Bristow Trophy**, the event was historically established to pit PDC champions against BDO/WDF champions. Today, it brings together PDC major winners, European Tour champions, and the top two female players from the PDC Women's Series.

## The Premier League: Darts' Biggest Arena Roadshow

Staged weekly from February to May across 16 major arenas in the UK, Ireland, Germany, and the Netherlands, **Premier League Darts** is the sport’s most commercially successful property.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
|                 PREMIER LEAGUE DARTS AT A GLANCE            |
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Field:         8 Elite Players                             |
|                 (Top 4 Order of Merit + 4 Wildcards)        |
|  Format:        16 Weekly Knockout Nights (Best of 11)      |
|  Points:        Night Winner (5 pts), Runner-Up (3 pts),    |
|                 Semi-Finalists (2 pts each)                 |
|  Play-Offs:     Top 4 advance to The O2, London             |
|                 Semi-Finals & Final on one dramatic night   |
|  Status:        NON-RANKING (Prize money does not affect    |
|                 PDC Order of Merit)                         |
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
```

While the Premier League does not count towards the Order of Merit, it offers £1,000,000 in total prize money (£275,000 to the champion) and exposes players to deafening arena crowds of up to 12,000 spectators every Thursday night.

## The "Triple Crown" of Professional Darts

In snooker, the Triple Crown consists of the World Championship, UK Championship, and Masters. In professional darts, the prestigious **Triple Crown** comprises the sport's three traditional pillar events:

1. **The PDC World Darts Championship** (Alexandra Palace)
2. **The World Matchplay** (Winter Gardens, Blackpool)
3. **Premier League Darts** (Play-Off Finals at The O2)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the biggest major tournament in darts?
The **PDC World Darts Championship** is the biggest and most lucrative tournament in the sport. Held from mid-December to early January at London's Alexandra Palace, it features a 128-player field, a record £5,000,000 prize fund, and awards £1,000,000 to the world champion along with the iconic Sid Waddell Trophy.

### Does Premier League Darts count toward the world rankings?
No. Premier League Darts is a non-ranking invitational tournament. While the champion receives £275,000, none of the prize money counts toward the official two-year rolling PDC Order of Merit.

### What is the difference between set play and leg play?
In **leg play** (used in the World Matchplay and Premier League), the first player to reach a target number of legs wins the match. In **set play** (used in the World Championship and World Grand Prix), players must win three legs to win a single set; the first player to win the designated number of sets wins the match. Set play allows a player to reset mentally between sets even after losing multiple legs.

### Why is the World Grand Prix unique?
The World Grand Prix is the only televised major in professional darts played under **Double-In, Double-Out** rules. Competitors must hit a double to begin scoring in each leg before finishing on a double to win the leg.

### What is the darts Triple Crown?
The Triple Crown consists of winning the sport's three most prestigious major titles: the PDC World Darts Championship, the World Matchplay, and Premier League Darts. Only three players in history have won all three: Phil Taylor, Michael van Gerwen, and Luke Humphries.
